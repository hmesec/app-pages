Roll Rescue — Support

Last updated: 1 September 2026

Roll Rescue does one job: it takes a .zip archive of your own photos and videos that is already on your iPhone, and puts the media inside it into your photo library — with the original capture dates intact, the right way up, once, and resumably. One screen, one job, entirely on your device.

It only adds. It never reads your library. Roll Rescue asks for add-only photo access, which is the permission to put items in and nothing more. It cannot see, search or count the photos you already have, and it never deletes anything.

How to use it

  • Get the archive onto your phone. Roll Rescue starts from a .zip file you already have — in Files, in iCloud Drive, or downloaded from wherever you exported it. The app does not fetch it and never signs in anywhere.
  • Pick it. Tap to choose the archive from the system file picker. If it is stored in iCloud and not yet on the phone, the app asks iOS to download it first and shows real byte progress while that happens.
  • Look at the scan. Before importing anything, the app reads the archive's index and tells you how many items it found and how big they are — plus the years they span, when the archive actually carries dates it can trust.
  • Watch the first 25 come back. The app imports the 25 oldest datable items into your photo library, free, and shows you their thumbnails. Open Photos and check the dates yourself before you decide anything.
  • Rescue the rest. Unlocking imports everything else in the archive. You can leave and come back — an interrupted run resumes where it stopped instead of starting over.

About the dates

Where does each date come from?
The file's own EXIF or QuickTime capture metadata first — that is the most reliable source, so the app hands the file to Photos and lets Photos read it. If the file carries no capture metadata, the app falls back to the archive's own index (converted from UTC), and then to the archive entry's modification time.
Some items came in without a date. Why?
Because nothing in the archive said when they were taken. Roll Rescue will not stamp them with today's date to make the import look tidy — it imports them anyway and lists them in the report by filename, so you can fix the ones you care about in Photos.
The scan didn't show a year range.
That means the archive's only usable dates were the file timestamps the export tool wrote on the day it was created, which say nothing about when the photos were taken. The app shows no range rather than an invented one.

Items the app skips, and why

  • A damaged entry. Every item is checksummed on the way out of the archive. If the checksum disagrees, the item is reported rather than written — a corrupt file in your library is worse than a known gap.
  • A password-protected archive. Roll Rescue cannot open encrypted entries and says so instead of failing silently. Re-export without a password.
  • An unusual compression method. Standard zip archives work; a few rarer methods (deflate64, bzip2, LZMA, zstd, XZ) are not decoded and are reported.
  • A sticker or caption layer shipped as its own file. When an archive stores an overlay separately from the picture it belongs to, the app burns it into the picture rather than importing it as a stray image. If that ever fails, the original media imports untouched and the report says so.

Space, time and interruptions

Do I need free space equal to the archive?
No. Items are unpacked one at a time and handed straight to Photos, so the extra space needed at any moment is roughly one item. The app checks you have room before it starts and tells you the numbers if you don't.
What happens if I close the app or it's interrupted?
Nothing is lost and nothing is done twice. The app keeps a private on-device log of what it has already imported, so reopening offers to resume the same archive from where it stopped.
Can I run it twice on the same archive?
Yes — items already imported are recognised and skipped rather than duplicated.

Your data & privacy

Roll Rescue has no accounts, no servers, and no analytics or tracking, and it contains no networking code of its own. Your archive and your media never leave your device. The App Store privacy label is Data Not Collected. Full details are in the Privacy Policy.

Common questions

Does it work offline?
Yes — apart from Apple's own App Store purchase, and iOS downloading your archive if you keep it in iCloud Drive rather than on the phone.
Will it delete or change my archive?
No. The archive is only read.
Does it touch the photos I already have?
No. Add-only access makes that impossible — the app cannot read your library at all.
Does it put everything in a named album?
No. Items go into your library with their own dates, so they land where they belong in your timeline rather than in a pile at the end.
