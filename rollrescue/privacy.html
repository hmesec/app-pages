Roll Rescue — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 September 2026

Roll Rescue takes a .zip archive of photos and videos that you already have on your iPhone and puts the media inside it into your photo library, with the original capture dates intact. This policy explains, in plain language, exactly what the app does and does not do with your information. The short version: Roll Rescue collects nothing about you, and it contains no networking code of its own.

No accounts, no servers, no tracking. Roll Rescue has no sign-in, no user account, and no server of its own. It includes no analytics, no crash reporter, no advertising and no third-party tracking software. We cannot see your archive, your photos, or anything the app did with them, because the app has no way to send them anywhere.

Your photo library: add-only, never read

Roll Rescue asks iOS for add-only access to your photo library — the permission that lets an app put items in and nothing more. That is the only photo permission it requests, and the only photo permission described in the app (NSPhotoLibraryAddUsageDescription).

Add-only access means the app cannot read, browse, search, or count the photos and videos you already have. It does not know what is in your library. It cannot tell you whether something is already there, and it never looks.

Your archive

You choose the archive yourself, from the system file picker. Roll Rescue reads it, unpacks one item at a time into its own temporary storage, and hands that item to Photos — which takes the file, leaving nothing behind. Your media is never copied anywhere else and never leaves your device.

If the archive you pick lives in iCloud Drive and is not yet downloaded to your iPhone, the app asks iOS to download it. That transfer happens between your device and Apple's iCloud, using your own iCloud account and your own file. We are not part of it and cannot see it.

What the app stores on your device

All of this is stored inside the app on your iPhone, and none of it is transmitted anywhere:

  • An import log. One line per item the app considered, recording that item's path inside the archive, its size and checksum, the capture date the app recovered and where that date came from, and what happened (imported, skipped, already done, failed). This is what lets an interrupted run resume where it stopped, lets a second run avoid importing the same item twice, and produces the summary you see at the end.
  • A bookmark to the archive you picked, so the app can offer to resume the same archive next time you open it.
  • A few small settings: whether a run stopped part-way, whether the unlock offer is still standing, and whether the app has already asked you to rate it.

Deleting the app removes all of it. You can also clear the archive bookmark simply by picking a different archive.

What we collect

Nothing. Specifically, Roll Rescue does not:

  • require or offer any account, email, or sign-in;
  • contain any analytics, advertising, attribution or crash-reporting software;
  • ask for permission to track you across apps or websites (there is no App Tracking Transparency prompt, because there is nothing to track);
  • access your location, contacts, camera, microphone, health data, or the photos already in your library;
  • connect to, log in to, or communicate with any online service — including the service your archive may have come from, and including any third-party AI service.

The app's PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy manifest declares no tracking, no tracking domains and no collected data types, and its App Store privacy label is Data Not Collected. That matches this page exactly.

Notifications

If your archive has to download from iCloud first, the app can offer to notify you when it is ready. If you accept, that notification is scheduled by iOS on your device. There is no push server, no device token, and nothing is registered with us or with anyone else. Declining costs you nothing but the reminder.

Encryption

Roll Rescue does not encrypt or decrypt anything, and it cannot open a password-protected archive. It computes SHA-256 hashes purely as local index keys for the import log described above. Its export-compliance declaration is ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption = false.

Purchases

Roll Rescue offers a single one-time in-app purchase (US$9.99) to unlock the full import. There is no subscription and no recurring charge. Payment is handled entirely by Apple through the App Store — we never see or store your payment details, and we receive no information that identifies you. Apple's handling of that transaction is governed by Apple's Privacy Policy.

Children

Roll Rescue is a general-purpose file utility and is not directed at children.
